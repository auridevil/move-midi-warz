At speed ×1 a step is a beat, so 4 steps = one bar; ×2 = eighths, ×4 = sixteenths, ½ = half notes. Lengths 3 · 4 · 5 · 7 at the same speed = polymeter; ratios ⅔ · 3/2 · 3 = true polyrhythm; 5/4 · 7/4 = fives and sevens over four beats, 4/3 = hemiola, ×6 = sixteenth triplets. Euclid spreads k hits evenly; rotation shifts them.

    
On the orbits: each planet is a hit you set, as solid as its chance of playing (a faint one seldom plays). A ring that flashes round a planet: it came round and stayed silent (solid ring: its chance said no · dotted: the locked dice said no · dashed: its condition). Arcs round a planet: a 1:3-style condition, one arc per loop from the top; the solid arc is the loop it plays on, the white one the loop you are in now. The thin arc outside the rings: how far you are through the whole cycle, until everything lines up again.

Ten short jobs you can finish in one to three minutes each, with the mouse and keys on the left and the Move on the right.


First five minutes
  1. Press play (or Space) and watch the sun count down the beats.
  2. Click a lane in the list at the top left: its knobs show along the bottom.
  3. Drag a knob up or down; hold Shift to go fine.
  4. Click a cell in the row of 16 to switch a step on or off.
  5. Click randomize for a new groove, and undo to take it back.
  6. Click all ⌘K, type a word, press Enter to find any control.
  7. Press Esc once to close whatever is open.
  8. Press ? for the keys and the Move tables.
  9. Press F to hide the controls; exit focus (or Esc) brings them back.
  10. Press the backquote key to see every number as plain text.
  11. Click share to copy a link to your beat.
  12. Stop with stop, the same ring that started it.


1. A beat in 60 seconds
Goal. Load a ready-made groove, hear it, change its sound and speed it up.
You need: nothing; the Move column is optional.
Mouse & keysMove
Click all ⌘K, type techno in find any control, press Enter twice (the first press finds the chip, the second loads it) → you see the four lanes become kick 909, rim, hat and sub, and a note: techno, 130 bpm—
Click play (or press Space) → you hear the beat, the ring shows a square and the counter beside it runs 01.1, 01.2, 01.3Play
Click kit (all lanes) and pick Trap → you hear every lane change its sound while the pattern stays the sameShift + ◀ ▶
Drag the tempo knob up → you hear it speed up, and a big number with "knob 8 · tempo" shows at the left while you hold itKnob 8
Click stop → you hear it stop and the ring shows a triangle againPlay
If it doesn't:
  • Nothing plays after the groove loads: a groove only loads the beat, so press play.
  • The kit box reads custom after a groove: that is only a name, pick a kit to replace every lane's sound.

Goal. Let the beat change itself each time the rings meet, then pull it back to where it began.
You need: nothing; the Move column is optional.
Mouse & keysMove

You need: nothing but a WAV or MP3 of a drum loop, up to 16 seconds; the Move column is optional.

  • The lane is silent: the lane's voice must read ♪ break, and picking another voice replaces it.
  • The loop is cut short: a file longer than 16 seconds keeps only its first 16 seconds, and a note says so.
Go further: Stems and MIDI into a DAW


8. Stems and MIDI into a DAW
Goal. Get one WAV per lane and a MIDI file to drop into your music program (a DAW).
You need: nothing; the Move has no export, so its column is empty.
Mouse & keysMove
Click all ⌘K, type house, press Enter twice → you see the house lanes—
Click all ⌘K, type export stems, press Enter once → you see the export tab open with ⤓ stems (.zip) outlined—
Click 24 beside format → you see 24 lit: the files will be 24-bit, a finer sound than 16—
Click render as loop to switch it off (on = a loop that repeats cleanly, off = a one-shot, a sound you place once) → you see the chip go dark—
Click ⤓ WAV  → you get the mix, and the note reads 4 bars + 2 bars ring-out at 124 bpm, one-shot · 24-bit 44.1 kHz—
Click ⤓ stems (.zip) → you get a zip with one WAV per lane plus the raw mix; each file carries its tempo and length inside (an ACID tag), so most DAWs line it up on their own—
Click ⤓ MIDI → you get a MIDI file, and the note reads a track per lane (click one track for a single drum-rack track)—
If it doesn't:
  • The stems end too early: with render as loop on, the tail wraps onto the start, so switch it off for the full tail.
  • Your DAW says 16-bit: the format is remembered, so click 24 before you export.
Go further: Share a loop


9. Share a loop
Goal. Send your beat as a link, and open one that someone sent you.
You need: nothing; the Move has no share button, so its column is empty.

Go further: The phone


10. The phone
Goal. Play and edit a beat with one thumb on a small screen.
You need: nothing but a phone; the Move column is empty.
Mouse & keysMove
Open the page on the phone → you see square rings across the width, a row of four colour dots, play, eight knobs and 16 cells, and the page never scrolls—
Tap play → you hear the beat and the button becomes stop—
Tap a colour dot, for example lane 2 clave → you see that dot outlined and its knobs take over—
Tap a cell twice → you see step 2, on and then step 2, on, accent (a tap goes off, on, accent)—
Press and hold a cell for a second (a long-press does what Shift, Alt and right-click do on a computer) → you see the header read step 3 with clear, more… and done—
Slide a finger up or down on a knob, for example tempo → you see a big number at the top left while you touch it and the beat speeds up—
Tap lane, then tap mute → you hear the outlined lane go quiet and its dot turns hollow—
Tap perform → you see fill hold, fill next, throw hold, undo, randomize, ● jam and share—
If it doesn't:
  • A tap on a cell did the wrong thing: one tap steps through off, on and accent, so tap again to reach the one you want.
  • A dot did not mute its lane: on the phone the lane dots only select, so mute from the lane tab.
